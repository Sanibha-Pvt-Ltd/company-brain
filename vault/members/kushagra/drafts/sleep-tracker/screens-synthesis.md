## Our first five minutes

1. **Open:** one sentence explaining what phone-only sleep tracking measures; no account, tracking prompt or score claim. [INFERRED]
2. **Choose setup:** set a usual bedtime/wake window or skip. Keep sleep schedule in one simple control. [INFERRED]
3. **Explain recording:** show why microphone/motion access is needed for the selected method, and offer a no-audio/manual fallback if product capability supports it. [INFERRED; technical feasibility UNKNOWN]
4. **Start tonight:** prominent start action and clear “tracking” state. No trial request here. [INFERRED]
5. **Next morning:** show the measured timeline, duration and interruptions; mark missing/estimated data. Then offer optional Health sync and explain what paid reports add. [INFERRED]

A real sleep result necessarily arrives after a night of capture; no screenshot establishes the exact time. The goal for the first five minutes is to start tracking with a clear benefit promised, not to fabricate an instant score. [INFERRED]

## Up to three differentiators

1. **Show the source of every result.** Several captures display a score or tailored insights without showing whether real sleep data produced them (ShutEye p. 31; Sleep Monitor pp. 17, 34; Leap p. 34). A report that labels measured, estimated and sample data would address this evidence gap. [INFERRED]
2. **Let the first night happen before the paywall.** Pillow p. 16, ShutEye pp. 25–28, Sleep Cycle pp. 10–11, Leap pp. 15–21 and SleepWatch pp. 15–21 show offers before a completed first night is evidenced. [OBSERVED] Offer paid coaching after a free morning report. [INFERRED]
3. **Forgiving recovery for missed/short nights.** The reviewed captures emphasize scores, streak-like seven-night framing or sleep-debt concepts (Remly p. 2; RISE pp. 35–40) but do not show how a failed recording is handled. A retry without guilt or score penalty is an opportunity inferred from that gap. [INFERRED]

## Concepts we refuse to add

- Multiple competing composite scores before showing measured time asleep. Several score/debt/energy frameworks appear across RISE pp. 35–46, ShutEye p. 31, Sleep Monitor p. 17 and Leap p. 34. [OBSERVED] Keep one optional score with clear meaning. [INFERRED]
- Disease-risk claims as onboarding bait; Sleep Monitor pp. 10–13 and Leap p. 13 lead with risk analysis before tracking. [OBSERVED] A consumer tracker should avoid diagnosis language. [INFERRED]
- Unrelated dream, sound, activity and wellness content in the critical path. These are visible across ShutEye pp. 39–40, Leap pp. 22–40 and RISE pp. 47–49. [OBSERVED]
- Account, broad Health access and cloud upload as universal prerequisites. Pillow pp. 6–12 and SleepWatch pp. 6–14 put multiple asks ahead of a tracked night. [OBSERVED] Request only when a chosen feature needs it. [INFERRED]
