# Sleep tracker: screens synthesis

App notes: [[members/kushagra/apps/sleep-tracker/rise]] · [[members/kushagra/apps/sleep-tracker/pillow]] · [[members/kushagra/apps/sleep-tracker/shuteye]] · [[members/kushagra/apps/sleep-tracker/sleep-monitor]] · [[members/kushagra/apps/sleep-tracker/sleepcycle]] · [[members/kushagra/apps/sleep-tracker/remly]] · [[members/kushagra/apps/sleep-tracker/leap-health]] · [[members/kushagra/apps/sleep-tracker/sleepwatch]].

**Main finding.** These captures sell interpretation before they demonstrate a measured night. Several show sleep scores, risk summaries, tailored plans or energy guidance, yet the captured pages do not establish that a night was recorded and produced those outputs. That creates a clear product decision: make a trustworthy first recording and its provenance the center of the first session; place scores and coaching after the user has evidence they can inspect. This conclusion is about the supplied screens, not user demand or competitor performance.

**Scope and evidence.** Screens-only review of eight supplied screenshot PDFs (279 pages). The PDF metadata creation date is 2026-10-03; screenshot capture dates are unknown except where a screen displays a date. Visible subscription amounts use INR; the screenshots do not establish storefront location, US prices, billing success, or current terms. Companion analysis PDF was not used as screenshot evidence. Each source page was visually inspected; offer details cited below were re-opened in larger renders, including RISE p. 50, Pillow p. 16, ShutEye p. 26, Sleep Monitor p. 23, Sleep Cycle p. 10, Remly p. 13 and SleepWatch p. 16. The per-app notes link to source filenames and page spans.

No historical hypotheses were supplied for this category. The hypotheses below are proposed here as `[INFERRED]` and assessed only against these captures; they are not prior research or market validation.

## Cross-app patterns and product implications

1. **Personalization arrives through a long sequence, but its evidence is uneven.** RISE asks about personal context and sleep practices before producing a profile and energy schedule (pp. 7–46); Pillow collects body/profile fields before a congratulatory transition (pp. 3–14); Remly turns selected focus areas into “Your personalized sleep plan” on its offer (p. 13). ShutEye and Sleep Monitor show numeric scores without a visible data-origin label (ShutEye p. 31; Sleep Monitor pp. 17, 34). The useful mechanism is not “more quiz = more personalization”; it is that user inputs can be reflected back in legible terms. The counterexample is a score or plan whose provenance is unclear. **Implication:** for Sanibha, echo only the input that visibly changes the next recommendation, and label estimates, defaults and recorded measurements distinctly. This should reduce setup concepts without claiming clinical accuracy. `[INFERRED]`

2. **Permission cost is front-loaded and method-specific asks are bundled.** Pillow's capture moves through notification, wearable, Motion, Health, microphone and iCloud topics before a sleep report appears (pp. 5–13). RISE explains Phone Motion and Apple Health before the profile outputs (pp. 23–28). Sleep Cycle asks for health-data consent near the beginning (pp. 1–4). Remly separates an ATT prompt (p. 1) from recorder choice and later Health/account/settings screens (pp. 3, 9–12). These are not equivalent permissions, and the screenshots do not establish which are technically required. **Exception:** some captures explain a specific ask, such as Pillow's privacy/safety screens (pp. 2, 13); context is better than an unexplained OS dialog. **Implication:** ask only for the recording method the user chooses, explain the immediate use before the OS prompt, and defer optional Health, notifications, account and cloud sync until the user reaches the corresponding feature. Test feasibility with engineering before promising phone-only or no-audio tracking. `[INFERRED]`

3. **A displayed “result” can precede evidence of a night.** ShutEye's 96 score, Sleep Monitor's 69/89 scores and Leap's 89 journal score are visible, but no cited screen proves the sleep was recorded in this run (ShutEye p. 31; Monitor pp. 17, 34; Leap p. 34). SleepWatch explicitly has a demo-data home (p. 22). RISE presents a “Building K's Profile” screen and personalized energy guidance after setup (pp. 29–46), but still no night report. **Exception:** Remly's “Tonight's Plan” is clearly a forward-looking plan rather than a measured sleep result (p. 2), making its scope less ambiguous. **Implication:** do not use a composite number as the first win unless its inputs and status are visible. First show “tracking is ready”; after recording, distinguish captured signals, estimates, and missing data. A demo can teach report structure if it is unmistakably labeled “example.” `[INFERRED]`

4. **The paid ask often precedes the capture's first observable report.** Pillow p. 16, Sleep Cycle pp. 10–11, Leap pp. 15–21 and SleepWatch pp. 15–21 place subscription surfaces before any completed night is shown. RISE places its offer after tailored setup (p. 50), but that is still not a measured-night result. ShutEye's paywall precedes its score explainer (pp. 26–31). **Counterexample:** Remly shows a home/Track path before its Pro offer (pp. 2–4, 13), though not a completed report. These placements establish screen order only, not a hard gate or whether another route exists. **Implication:** make the first night and a basic morning summary available before asking for payment; monetize continued coaching, richer history or optional content after demonstrating a credible report. `[INFERRED]`

5. **There are useful ideas buried in broad feature bundles.** RISE's schedule/energy curve (pp. 31–46), Remly's Tonight plan and single central Track action (pp. 2–4), and Leap's journal/trend and editable factors (pp. 34–43) are concrete product patterns. In contrast, ShutEye and Leap include risk, dream, sound, stage, activity and other modules in the same overall flows (ShutEye pp. 35–45; Leap pp. 22–46). Pillow offers sound recording and optional cloud sharing, but those are not prerequisites established by the screenshots. **Implication:** Sanibha should choose one primary job—record, understand, improve—and make secondary content opt-in destinations. Keep one interpretable report path rather than importing each competitor's menu. `[INFERRED]`

## First five minutes: proposed experience

The five-minute target is to reach a clear “ready to record” state, not to promise a sleep result before a night has elapsed. Copy below is original direction, not competitor text or a verified product capability. Any recording method, sensor access and local processing promise requires implementation validation.

| Step | Screen and proposed copy direction | Ask | What the user gets | Why this order; evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Welcome:** “Understand your sleep with a report from your own recording.” Avoid scores, risk claims and instant-result promises. | One continue action. | Clear job and a boundary: report follows a recording. | Scores/plans precede measured evidence in multiple captures (ShutEye p. 31; Monitor p. 17; Remly p. 13). `[INFERRED]` |
| 2 | **Choose recording setup:** present only methods confirmed by engineering; label what each collects and what it needs. | Choose one method, or leave setup. | User understands the next permission before seeing an OS prompt. | Pillow bundles Watch, Motion, Health, microphone and cloud asks (pp. 5–12); exact requirements are unknown. `[INFERRED]` |
| 3 | **Permission explanation:** one concise, method-specific explanation, followed by that method's system prompt. | Selected permission only. | Consent tied to an action the user just chose. | RISE explains motion/Health (pp. 23–28); Pillow has privacy/safety context (pp. 2, 13). `[INFERRED]` |
| 4 | **Tonight setup:** optional bedtime/wake window and a visible start/ready control; no broad quiz. | Set a schedule or skip; start recording when appropriate. | A prepared recording and an obvious state. | RISE's weekday/weekend schedule is concrete (pp. 31–34); Remly's Track CTA is prominent (pp. 2–4). `[INFERRED]` |
| 5 | **Before sleep:** concise placement/setup instructions relevant to the selected method, with help and stop controls. | Confirm readiness. | Confidence that recording is active; no score invented. | Captures show pairing/setup or tracking-start instructions (Pillow pp. 11–14; Leap p. 22), but no reliability evidence. `[INFERRED]` |

## Up to three differentiated proposals

1. **A provenance-first morning report.** Show what was recorded, what is estimated, and what is unavailable beside duration and timeline. Evidence: scores appear without clear source in ShutEye p. 31, Sleep Monitor pp. 17/34, and Leap p. 34; SleepWatch p. 22 is explicitly demo data. Tradeoff: more explanation and potentially less dramatic first-screen impact than a single score. Falsifier: users consistently prefer a concise score and can correctly explain what data generated it in a comprehension test. `[INFERRED]`
2. **Permission requests that follow a chosen method.** Separate core recording setup from optional Health, Watch, notifications, cloud and sound features. Evidence: stacked asks in Pillow pp. 5–12 and RISE pp. 23–28; Remly asks for recorder choice p. 3 and later account/settings pp. 9–12. Tradeoff: multiple branches and engineering complexity; permissions may be mandatory for a chosen mode. Falsifier: a technically validated path cannot record useful sleep without the permissions we planned to defer, or users understand a single bundled consent better. `[INFERRED]`
3. **One interpretation layer, after raw sleep evidence.** Start with the timeline/duration; offer one explainable schedule or trend, then optional coaching/content. Evidence: RISE's schedule/energy output pp. 31–46, Remly's Tonight plan pp. 2–4, Leap's journal/factors pp. 34–43, alongside larger feature bundles in ShutEye/Leap. Tradeoff: postpones content discovery that may be central to a chosen business model. Falsifier: a prototype test shows sound, dream or coaching content is the primary job users return for and the core recording path still remains clear. `[INFERRED]`

## Prioritized open questions

1. **Can the selected setup produce a reliable report without optional permissions?** On a fresh install, record a full night under each proposed method; log exactly which permissions, hardware placement and network conditions are required. This decides the setup branches and privacy explanation.
2. **What is measured versus inferred?** Inspect raw signals and compare the morning report against a controlled reference. This decides whether the first report leads with duration, stages, interruptions, score or an explicit “insufficient data” state.
3. **Does a basic report create enough value before payment?** Prototype report-first versus offer-first; measure recording completion and report comprehension, not just trial starts. This could change paywall placement.
4. **Which concept earns a return visit?** Test schedule guidance, trends and one coaching feature across multiple nights. Screenshots show these surfaces but do not establish repeat use or preference.
5. **What are actual reminders, repeat costs, trial settings and dismissal paths?** Hands-on fresh-install testing with timed recording; screenshots cannot reveal taps, delays, hidden exits, or hard/soft gates.
6. **Do users value sleep-risk/dream/sound features, and what claims can be substantiated?** Review mining and product/legal/clinical evidence are required before including any such promise. No demand inference is made here.

## What would change this call / next stage

If recording requires the broad permissions currently bundled by Pillow/RISE, we should explain that necessity and compare setup methods rather than promise a minimal-ask path. If score comprehension testing shows users understand and prefer an aggregate metric, keep it as a summary but retain visible provenance. If the first-night report is not trusted or not differentiated, test the schedule/plan as an intermediate benefit without presenting it as measured sleep. If hands-on tests show users can reach a meaningful report with little friction and the paywall is consistently optional, the report-first recommendation becomes less distinctive and should be judged on user outcomes.

**Next stage:** hands-on fresh-install and overnight capture for at least two representative methods; verify report provenance, permission requirements, recovery after failed recording, and repeat-session flow. Then test comprehension and paywall placement. Market demand, pricing fit and review sentiment remain outside this screens-only pass.
