# Focus apps: screen synthesis

Seven supplied in-app screenshot PDFs, 252 pages. This set is intentionally heterogeneous: Finch is a self-care companion with goals; Flora, Focus Traveller, Focus Friend and Study Bunny foreground different timer/companion experiences; Focus Plant and Forest show focus sessions with app-blocking or reward systems. This is not evidence that all seven compete for the same user or solve the same job. `Focus_App_Analysis.pdf` is secondary, unverified commentary and is not used as evidence. No web, review or market research was conducted. Prices are transcribed in visible currency only; `₹` does not establish an Apple storefront. Capture date and US prices are unknown.

**Strongest evidence-bounded insight [OBSERVED]:** the captures show many ways to make focus feel tangible—timer, journey, bean/room, plant/tree, bunny, self-care pet—but rarely show enough of a continuous session to verify its actual outcome. Focus Traveller has a short timer/rest scene (p.12–14) while its separate stats pages report zero sessions (p.20–23); Focus Friend shows running timer controls (p.16–18) and zero sessions/minutes (p.20); Flora reports zero hours/real trees (p.19); Study Bunny has a 30-minute timer tutorial (p.5) and a “Great Work!” reward screen (p.6), but the captures do not prove these are the same run. Therefore the evidence supports conclusions about onboarding, promises and UI states—not session completion, sustained focus, retention or the effectiveness of blocking.

## Patterns and product implications

1. **“Focus” is several jobs with different first wins.** Finch’s first shown value is an emotionally framed pet and self-care goal (p.1–40), not a conventional timer. Focus Plant starts from phone-use awareness and choosing whether to lock apps (p.4–6, p.17). Forest adds tree growth, blocking and a shared forest (p.2–6, p.18). Flora, Traveller, Friend and Study Bunny put a timer, journey or study companion closer to the center. This is a counterexample to a single category funnel. Product implication [INFERRED]: treat the initial product choice as a research question. For the prototype below, choose a timer as the primary job with optional blocking; do not build a three-mode product menu from this sample alone.

2. **A visible timer is not the same as a completed focus session.** Focus Friend shows a running/control state p.16–18 but zero-session stats p.20; Traveller has timer/rest UI p.12–14 and zero-session stats p.20–23; Study Bunny pairs a tutorial timer p.5 with reward copy p.6, but continuity is unproven. Flora’s stats p.19 report zero hours/real trees. These pages are useful precisely because they expose the difference between designed feedback and verified outcome. Counterexample: app stats may be empty because the capture was early or seeded state, not because sessions fail. Product implication [INFERRED]: preserve a clear session state machine, and award progress only when the timer/session records a completion; separately label tutorial/demo feedback.

3. **System permissions are justified by different value promises, but capture order alone does not prove necessity.** Forest explains Screen Time access near distraction setup (p.2–5); Focus Plant asks about device-use patterns and presents a Screen Time/report flow (p.4–6); Flora asks for task creation and later settings include tree behavior (p.5, p.17). These permissions can support blocking or reporting. Counterexample: a user can still benefit from a timer without blocking or phone-use analysis. Product implication [INFERRED]: explain the concrete capability unlocked, offer a timer-only path, and request Screen Time only when the user opts into blocking/reporting. Test actual OS behavior before promising enforcement.

4. **Companions and objects can make an abstract session emotionally legible, at different complexity costs.** Finch’s pet reciprocity and world (p.9–11, p.30–40), Focus Friend’s bean/room/scarf progression (p.9–16), Study Bunny’s tutor and reward language (p.2–6), Focus Plant’s plant/water (p.18–25), Forest’s tree/forest (p.6, p.18) and Traveller’s journey/rest landscape (p.12–14) are not interchangeable skins. Some carry currencies, shops or social surfaces; Flora has tree loss language at p.17. Counterexample: Flora and Traveller represent feedback without Finch’s larger self-care quiz/world. Product implication [INFERRED]: choose one coherent metaphor aligned to the selected job, keep it peripheral to timer controls, make its progress follow recorded user action, and only then test whether reminders, history or daily challenges create a useful return loop (Finch p.36–40; Forest p.18–33; Focus Friend p.20–28). These return surfaces are pictured, but no later-day return is evidenced. Whether a character or return loop improves retention is unknown.

5. **Reward and interruption rules materially change the emotional contract.** Flora’s settings say “Tree killed if you left the app” (p.17); Focus Plant says sessions under five minutes receive no rewards (p.31). Forest’s tree is visible at 0:01 (p.6), but the screenshot does not establish what happens when interrupted. Focus Friend’s timer offers controls p.16–18; Study Bunny’s reward screen p.6 shows a reward state, not a documented failure rule. Counterexamples mean no general claim about category punishment is justified. Product implication [INFERRED]: specify pause, interruption, and partial-credit behavior before start; prototype preserving partial effort and test whether that reduces avoidance without undermining the chosen commitment mechanic.

6. **The paid surfaces include both subscriptions and optional one-off purchases; offer states can vary.** Traveller’s Pro page p.8, Friend’s shop p.21, Forest’s early-bird annual page p.12–14, Focus Plant p.13/p.27 and Finch p.34/p.57–58 show different purchase frames; Flora’s tours p.21–22 are one-off cards and Study Bunny p.26 is an IAP list. Finch and Focus Plant captured annual offers differ, but the captures do not prove one user saw both or whether personalization/storefront accounts for differences. Product implication [INFERRED]: price the selected value clearly at the moment of intent; test plan consistency in native purchase sheets. Screens alone say nothing about conversion or willingness to pay.

## Differentiators to test (maximum three)

1. **Choose a primary job before expanding modes.** Evidence: Finch’s self-care path p.12–40 differs from Plant’s phone-use questions p.4–6 and Forest’s tree/blocking path p.2–6. The supplied sample cannot tell us whether these should be separate products or settings in one app. Tradeoff: a broad product could blur its promise; splitting too early could fragment value. Falsifier: hands-on interviews show timer and interruption-control users naturally expect one integrated flow.
2. **Explicit interruption contract with optional blocking.** Evidence: Flora’s “Tree killed if you left the app” p.17, Plant’s under-five-minute no-reward rule p.31, and Plant lock choice p.17. Tradeoff: preserving partial credit could weaken users’ preferred strict commitment. Falsifier: users seeking strict enforcement find the optional/forgiving rule less useful; actual platform testing shows interruptions are rare/manageable.
3. **Feedback only after a recorded focus session.** Evidence: timer and stats states in Traveller p.12–14/p.20–23 and Friend p.16–20; Study Bunny tutorial/reward screenshots p.5–6 are not verified same-run evidence. Tradeoff: less immediate animation during onboarding and fewer reward moments. Falsifier: a prototype test shows tutorial reward improves comprehension without being confused for real progress.

## Open questions, prioritized by decision impact

1. **Which product job should we build first?** Test prototype entry choices with people who seek timer focus, app interruption reduction and self-care support separately; observe task understanding and choice. Decision: whether to build one mode or distinct product paths. Screens cannot resolve audience overlap.
2. **What is the real session lifecycle?** On a clean install, record start, pause, app switch, interruption, early end, completion and history for Flora, Traveller, Friend, Plant, Forest and Study Bunny; verify whether the pictured states are reachable in the expected order. Decision: timer state model and what feedback is earned.
3. **What does Screen Time permission actually enable?** Hands-on test on the target OS: allow/deny, selected apps, backgrounding, interruption, revocation and fallback. Decision: whether blocking is optional, reliable and accurately described.
4. **What is the first genuine outcome and its repeat path for each intent?** For self-care, complete one goal in Finch; for timers, complete and restart a session; for phone-use reduction, complete a lock session and inspect report. Capture later-day return and record taps only from actual video. Decision: first-win scope, day-two prompt and repeat cost.
5. **What are the canonical purchase terms?** For each offer, select plan and inspect native sheet, renewal period, trial length, eligibility and cancel terms. Reconcile Finch p.34/p.57–58 and Plant p.13/p.27. Decision: product pricing presentation only; current storefront pricing remains outside this note.
6. **What user preference exists around strict vs forgiving sessions?** Prototype interruption choices; test strict lock, pause, partial progress and restart with timer users. Decision: default penalty/forgiveness policy. Screens alone do not show user sentiment.
7. **Are the displayed efficacy/user-count claims supportable?** Independently verify before reuse (Plant p.8–9; Forest p.12). Decision: whether any such statement belongs in our product or marketing. No claim here endorses them.

## What would change this call

If audience testing shows timer, app blocking and self-care users want one integrated entry, replace the intent split with a shared home and optional modes. If hands-on sessions demonstrate that interruption destroys the session by design and users explicitly prefer strictness, make the strict rule an opt-in commitment instead of preserving partial progress by default. If repeated real sessions show companion feedback is distracting or is not understood as earned, remove or quieten it. These are update rules, not claims established by the screenshots.

**Next stage:** hands-on session/permission and clean-install offer captures, then choose the first product intent and prototype its verified outcome. Reviews, retention, market size, causal efficacy and current prices remain out of scope for this screens-only note.
