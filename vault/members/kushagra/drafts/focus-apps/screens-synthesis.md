## Our first five minutes

This is a screen-by-screen product proposal, not a claim about observed competitor timing.

1. **Choose the task.** One text field (“What do you want to focus on?”), with a skip option. No profile, pet traits, questionnaire or account.
2. **Choose the session.** Duration selector with a sensible default and an option to change it; show Start. Do not gate the first session behind a subscription screen.
3. **Offer app blocking only if wanted.** Explain Screen Time access in one sentence and let the user proceed without it. This borrows the direct rationale in Forest p.5 and Focus Plant’s with/without-lock choice p.17 while avoiding required setup before any focus.
4. **Run a calm session.** Show remaining time and Pause/End; preserve partial effort if interrupted. Avoid failure states such as “tree killed” (Flora p.17) or “no rewards” for a short session (Focus Plant p.31).
5. **Show earned progress.** Confirm minutes actually recorded and a small companion/plant change. Then offer a reminder, account save, or paid plan as optional next steps. A first reward screen is visible in Study Bunny p.6, but its linkage to the selected timer is not verified.

## Differentiators supported by the screenshots

1. **A focus session before intake.** Finch’s profile and support-area questions occupy p.13–29 before its starter plan at p.30–31; Focus Plant asks use and Screen Time questions before timer p.4–6. A useful timer first can reduce commitment before the user knows the core value. [INFERRED]
2. **Forgiving interruption handling.** Flora says “Tree killed if you left the app” (p.17), and Focus Plant says sessions under five minutes get no rewards (p.31). Preserve progress and offer a restart/pause path. [INFERRED]
3. **A character that recognizes real effort.** Focus Friend’s scarf/room (p.9–15) and Finch’s self-care companion (p.9–10, p.30) show distinct emotional hooks. Make the response follow an actually completed session or checked-off action rather than a tutorial step. [INFERRED]

## Concepts we refuse to add

- **Long sensitive intake before a first task.** Finch’s multiple mental-health and lifestyle questions (p.13–29) are too much upfront; tailor after the first action.
- **Loss punishment for interruption.** “Tree killed if you left the app” (Flora p.17) and no reward under five minutes (Focus Plant p.31) risk making an interruption feel like failure.
- **Several currencies and shops in the core loop.** Focus Plant’s raindrops/land/plant loop (p.20–25), Forest coins/crystals (p.39–41), and Study Bunny coins/carrots (p.6, p.26) add conversions and shopping tasks.
- **Mandatory social/progress systems.** Don’t require friends, leaderboards, streak commitments, pet naming or groups to start a solo timer (Finch p.38–45; Traveller p.5, p.19–24; Focus Friend p.28).
- **Ambiguous or repeated trials.** Finch has different trial/renewal displays in the supplied captures (p.34, p.57–59); Focus Plant shows different annual offers (p.13, p.27). Use one offer per user state and show exact renewal terms consistently.

## Unknowns and verification plan

- **Real onboarding sequence:** Run each app from clean install to first timer and first completed session; record exact asks, paths, taps, ability to skip, and any paywall close action.
- **System access:** Confirm when Screen Time access is actually needed, whether permission flows are duplicated, and what blocking state looks like on interruption.
- **Outcome and interruption behavior:** Complete a session, leave the app, return early, and observe timer persistence, plant/pet consequence, rewards and history.
- **Trial terms:** Reconcile the different Finch offers and Focus Plant annual offers using the native purchase sheet for each displayed selection. Displayed ₹ or USD currency alone does not establish storefront context.
- **Repeat behavior:** Record real taps to start again, change task, inspect history, and resume after an interruption.
- **Evidence not present here:** Reviews, retention, whether reported usage changes are causal, and whether any claimed user counts or outcomes are accurate require separate research and were intentionally not inferred.
