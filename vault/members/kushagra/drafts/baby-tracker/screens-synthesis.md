## Our first five minutes (proposal)

1. **Landing:** Start with four large actions: Feed, Sleep, Diaper, Pump. Add “I’m expecting” as a secondary route. No account ask. [INFERRED from common logging categories across all ten apps]
2. **First log:** Open the chosen category directly to a minimal event form. Pre-fill current time; offer a one-tap start/stop for sleep and feeding duration. Save locally. Confirm “Saved” with event and time. [INFERRED; event categories observed broadly]
3. **Dashboard:** Show the new event immediately in today’s timeline and keep “Log another” prominent. Do not present an empty chart as insight. [INFERRED from repeated empty-state/dashboard screens]
4. **Optional child profile:** Ask name and birth date only when needed for age-based views; allow skip and edit later. Explain the benefit beside the ask. [INFERRED from profile questions across apps]
5. **Optional sync:** After the first saved event, offer caregiver sharing/account. Explain that local logging works without it. Ask notifications only when the user turns on reminders. [INFERRED from collaboration/notification screens]
6. **Honest monetization:** After several useful records or when a paid-only action is tapped, show a single clear plan screen. Give plan duration, total renewal amount/date, trial length, and cancel instructions together; include a visible close and usable free logging. [INFERRED from observed paywall examples; selection of trigger requires product decision]

## Differentiators to test (maximum three)

- **One-tap logging before identity asks:** Several apps gate dashboard access behind child details, login, invites, or onboarding (BabyConnect pp.2-14; Huckleberry pp.8-21; Napper pp.2-25; Tottli pp.1-19) [OBSERVED]. A first useful log without sign-up is a plausible gap [INFERRED]; validate hands-on.
- **Shared care with a single child timeline:** Family/caregiver sharing appears repeatedly (BabyConnect pp.5-6; Huckleberry pp.31-32; Nappi pp.6-7; Nara Baby pp.11,24; Pebbi pp.17-18) [OBSERVED]. Make attribution and handoff legible on every event, avoiding separate family concepts [INFERRED].
- **Honest prediction boundaries:** Sleep predictions are prominent (Huckleberry pp.28-29; Napper pp.19-23/41-45; Nappi pp.16; Pebbi pp.16/24) [OBSERVED]. Show the underlying logged inputs, confidence/range, and an easy way to ignore/correct; never promise clinical outcomes [INFERRED]. Needs domain and safety review before productization.

## Concepts to refuse

- No streaks, guilt, or implied parental failure for missed logs: not needed for the tracking job [INFERRED].
- No forced “leaps,” routines, modes, or proprietary sleep vocabulary before logging; explain any prediction in plain language [INFERRED from observed concept layers].
- No account/invite/notification wall before first saved event [INFERRED].
- No trial button labeled “free” without total renewal price and charge date directly beside it; reject trial flows whose plan confirmation is unclear [INFERRED from observed India paywall examples].
- No broad health or AI claims as a substitute for useful records; keep advice claims separately verified [INFERRED].

## Unknowns / verification

- Exact taps, elapsed time, backtracking, permission denial paths, and paywall dismissal outcomes: require hands-on device walkthroughs.
- US storefront pricing, offer eligibility, paywall selection defaults, and billing behavior: not established by India-region screenshots.
- Whether screen labels represent working features, predictions’ quality, reminder timing, offline behavior, caregiver sync reliability, widgets, and smartwatch support: screenshots cannot establish functionality.
- Which first log matters most (feed vs sleep vs diaper) and whether caregivers value predictions over simple coordination: use short parent interviews/usability sessions and event analytics.
- Whether the displayed claims such as “science-backed,” “expert-vetted,” or “personalized” are substantiated: out of screenshot scope; separately verify before copying.

## What would change the call

A hands-on test showing families consistently prefer completing profile/account setup before recording anything would weaken the “log first” proposal. Reliable evidence that caregivers choose a prediction-led workflow over fast logging would raise the priority of a transparent sleep-guidance differentiator. US pricing and paywall experiments could change monetization placement. Review mining and usability evidence are not included in this screenshot-only pass.
