## 0. Executive brief

**Company thesis.** Build a global, India-operated, performance-marketing-led consumer software company. Repeatedly **find → build → launch → measure → scale/kill → repeat** narrowly focused apps with proven demand, weak brand affinity, immediate time-to-value, measurable unit economics, and shared technical and commercial infrastructure. AI reduces build cost; **distribution, creative execution, product judgment and capital allocation** remain the scarce capabilities.

**Founder edge.** The prospective founder has approximately 12+ years of marketing/business/P&L experience, has led teams of 100+, managed >$500M in marketing budgets, scaled businesses to >200M installs, and worked on positive-unit-economics growth. Most relevant roles include CMO/CBO-level responsibilities in gaming. These are **founder-supplied** credentials that should be checked for consistency before external investor use.

**Initial anchor.** US iOS subscription utilities, particularly **storage/photo management**, **TV remote**, and—depending on the research snapshot—**focus improvement**, **document scanner/PDF** or **AI calorie tracking**. **No universally finalized three-app launch sequence exists across the research record**; see decision history below.

**Core test.** Can a paid-acquired installation return its acquisition spend by D30 **after** refunds, store fees and variable costs while retaining meaningful subscriber value into later months? This metric, called **CRR30** in the project, is more important than downloads, headline ARR or gross ROAS. Test D7, D30, renewal, cohort quality, marginal CPI and cash settlement delay.

**Geographies.** US is the primary absolute-scale market. Western Europe (especially Germany/France/Italy/Spain), Saudi/UAE, UK/Canada/Australia, and Japan are plausible incremental iOS markets. Android deserves its **own** economics and product plan; Germany-led Western Europe and South Korea look comparatively attractive for subscription monetization, while Brazil/Mexico are potentially **lower-RPI, high-volume** plays.

**Year-1 financing envelope (planning recommendation, not a requirement).** For three properly tested apps across North America, UK, Australia, Western Europe and GCC, earlier discussions suggested **₹7.5–10 crore** total capital availability, with a working target of **~₹8 crore** and **~₹4.5–6 crore paid-UA reserve**. Capital must be released through validation gates, not spent on schedule. The precise staffing/business-model plan must be updated once the founders commit and actual salaries (possibly zero) are agreed.

**Funding history.** A September investor memo used **$1M at $10M pre-money**. Later negotiation advice suggested opening at **$8M pre-money**, targeting **$7–8M**, and considering **$6–7M** depending on investor quality—**advice, not a current priced market quote or agreed term sheet**. These versions should not be conflated. $1M at $8M pre-money gives the investor ~11.1% post-money, before any option-pool reshuffling.

**Founding equity.** A trusted CTO candidate with >15 years' experience and three years of direct working history was discussed. With **zero salary** and genuine full-time cofounder responsibility, the latest working offer was **20% with a path/negotiating willingness to 25%**, subject to vesting; without cofounder-level commitment, different economics apply. Growth-head and analytics-head **zero-salary equity were asked about but not finalized**; do not infer grants.

## 1. Business model and strategic constraints

### 1.1 What the company is

- **WORKING:** Global consumer-app publisher/operating platform built predominantly from India, initially targeting high-income geographies.
- **DECIDED in the September investor narrative:** "US-first • iOS-first • India-built."
- Brand/parent-company naming exploration has included **Sanibha**; final brand/legal name is not established in this record.
- The company is not an IT-services shop, general AI wrapper collection, or a one-app bet by design, but **must be willing to become hero-first** if one app earns the right to receive most resources.
- Rather than creating a new consumer behavior, capture existing user intent or demonstrate a job-to-be-done instantly.

### 1.2 What constitutes a good category

Initial eight-filter checklist:

1. Large consumer demand.
2. Meaningful willingness to spend.
3. Several companies can monetize; not winner-take-all.
4. New entrants can still break through.
5. Paid acquisition demonstrably works in the category.
6. Good fit with US iOS/high-income consumer markets.
7. Brand affinity and network effects weak enough for a challenger.
8. Achievable **launch-grade** feature parity.

Three later filters added:

9. Legitimate repeat need / Month-2+ retention.
10. Low/managed Apple/Google/native-feature substitution risk.
11. Acceptable ongoing variable costs (AI inference, carrier APIs, fax routing, etc.).

**Also evaluate:** keyword intent, permission friction, time-to-value, privacy/trust, refund exposure, customer support load, content moderation/telecom/compliance burden, geographic portability, Android feasibility, and ability to recycle paid-acquisition cash at D7/D30.

### 1.3 Portfolio operating doctrine

- **Discover quickly, build launch-grade, measure ruthlessly, fund only evidence.**
- Avoid ten shallow apps. Prefer ~3 serious products and a possible fourth only if the first three have not revealed a winner.
- One shared tech, event, attribution, creative and experimentation system; each new app should be faster and cheaper to ship.
- Paid Search/Apple Ads suits **urgent/high-intent** products (remote, cleaner, scanner); Meta/TikTok can suit visual-demo or behavior-change products (calorie, interior, focus).
- Cross-promotion and bundles can later lower effective portfolio CAC; account for the **opportunity cost** of inventory/traffic diverted from a paying app.
- Monetization must be transparent. Avoid misleading weekly-paywall design, fake device-cleaning claims, obscured renewal terms or unauthorised data access.
- Scale only **marginal** contribution economics, not blended averages distorted by old cheap cohorts.

## 4. Storage/photo management: launch-grade product specification

### 4.1 Positioning

- **Acquisition term:** “Storage Cleaner” / "phone cleaner" / "clean iPhone" / local-language variants.
- **Product promise, first session:** “Get your storage back.”
- **Long-term promise:** “Never let your iPhone become cluttered again.”
- **Working product identity:** **Storage Autopilot / Memory Cleaner**—from deleting noise to protecting memories and establishing a regular cleanup habit.
- **Primary day-0 north star:** percentage of new users who genuinely free **≥1GB** in the first session (with a separate measure for users who **could** reclaim ≥1GB; avoid penalizing small libraries).

### 4.2 Core architecture: five pillars

1. **Storage intelligence:** inventory, reclaimable-space estimate, storage dashboard, transparent progress.
2. **Smart cleanup:** exact duplicates, visually similar shots, blurry/low-quality detection, screenshots, screen recordings, oversized video and similar videos.
3. **Compression:** video compression quality options and before/after previews, batch workflows, photo compression where appropriate.
4. **Photo organization:** best-shot selection, swipe cleanup, defer/decide later, On This Day, smart screenshot categories, event/trip clusters, protect people/favorites/albums.
5. **Storage Autopilot:** new-since-last-clean, weekly cleanup, monthly report, storage-health score, cleanup history, widget and habit prompts.

Additional possibilities: duplicate contact cleanup + backup; calendar cleanup; private vault; memory map. **Do not conflate optional features with day-one requirements**; the launch should be competitive and trustworthy, not bloated.

### 4.3 Retention loops

| Loop | Reason to return | Risk / design requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly cleanup | New images/videos/screenshots accumulate | Real useful space savings; no artificial scare tactics |
| Memories / On This Day | Emotional re-engagement | Do not surface sensitive or unwanted memories without controls |
| Storage score / history | Progress and visibility | Show real metrics, not manipulated urgency |
| Trip/event cleanup | Post-event media bursts | Respect protected people/albums and permissions |

### 4.4 Non-negotiable trust and platform reality

- Analyze on device where feasible; do not upload photos by default without clear permission/disclosure.
- **Never delete without user review and confirmation.** Protect favorites/hidden/protected content.
- Show how much storage is *potentially* recoverable vs *actually* free.
- On iOS, deleted photos move to **Recently Deleted**; physical space may not be reclaimed until that is emptied. Explain transparently.
- **No fake RAM booster, system-cache cleaner, or other-app cache deletion claims.** iOS sandboxing prevents generic access to other apps' internals.
- App cannot promise direct access to WhatsApp/other apps' private data; may operate only on user-authorized Photos assets or supported shared inputs.
- Email cleaning via Gmail OAuth restricted scopes may trigger verification/security-assessment obligations; consider it an optional separate workstream.
- Performance QA at library sizes ~1K / 10K / 25K / 50K / 100K / 200K photos; progressive results, resumable scans, thermal/memory safety, low crash rate.
- Product should show useful results before monetization where feasible; test scan → “we found X GB” → ethical paywall vs limited free action.

### 4.5 Key risks / what is unproven

- Category demand, monetization and incumbent paid-UA activity are strong; **our CAC and CRR30 are unknown**.
- One-off cleanup may destroy retention without Autopilot/Memory Curator loops.
- Requesting full Photos access can depress activation. iOS Photos limited-access choices require a thoughtful flow.
- Aggressive, predatory weekly paywalls may produce high early revenue but refunds, churn, complaints and regulatory/platform risk.
- Different device storage constraints and photo-library compositions affect activation; segment by potential recoverable GB.

## 6. Monetization, funnel and unit-economics dictionary

**Unit of analysis:** acquisition **cohort × app × country × platform × channel × campaign × creative × paywall**. Do not compare countries on CPI or monthly revenue totals alone.

| Term | Definition | Mistake to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| CPI | paid acquisition spend ÷ attributed installs | Country benchmark ≠ category-specific or marginal CPI |
| CPT | Apple Ads spend ÷ taps | CPT ≠ CPI; tap→install conversion matters |
| Install→trial | trial starts ÷ installs | Paid subscription offers without trial need separate path |
| Trial→paid | first paid conversions ÷ trial starts | Trial cohorts require enough maturity to observe conversion |
| Install→paid | payers ÷ installs | Mix of direct purchases and trial conversions |
| **Gross D30 RPI** | app sales billed/recognized from acquisition cohort by D30 ÷ paid installs | Never compute by current-month revenue ÷ current-month installs |
| **Net D30 RPI** | (gross D30 sales − platform fee − refunds − attributable variable costs) ÷ paid installs | Fee rate differs by program and subscription tenure |
| **CRR30** | **cohort D30 net contribution ÷ cohort acquisition spend** | Net sales collected/reported may settle after D30 |
| CAC/payer | acquisition spend ÷ paying customers | A cheap CPI with poor conversion can have expensive payer CAC |
| Payer LTV | lifetime customer revenue (specify gross/net, discounted/undiscounted) ÷ payers | Do not replace cohort RPI with payer LTV |
| Retention | specify user vs subscriber; D7/D30, paid first renewal, annual renewal | D30 behavioral retention ≠ subscription retention |
| Marginal CRR30 | incremental D30 contribution ÷ incremental UA spend at the new spend level | Blended economics often hide scaling decay |

### Main formulas

```text
Gross D30 RPI = Gross D30 cohort revenue / paid installs

Net D30 RPI = [Gross D30 cohort revenue × (1 - store fee)
              - refunds - variable COGS] / paid installs

CRR30 = Net D30 RPI / fully loaded acquisition CPI

Payer CAC = CPI / install-to-paid conversion rate

Required gross D30 RPI ≈ CPI / retained contribution share
```

**Fee caution:** 30% can be a conservative store-fee planning case; 15% may apply under qualifying program/renewal rules, depending on store, subscriber tenure, revenue thresholds, geography and rules as of launch. Verify actual Apple/Google program eligibility, taxes, settlement, currency and cancellation/refund treatment; do not assume a universal 30% or 15%.

### Illustrative comparison from previous discussion

If CPI is **$1.50** and **75% of gross cohort revenue remains** after fees/refunds/variable costs, breakeven gross D30 RPI is ~$2.00. At CPI **$4.00**, it is ~$5.33. This illustrates why Saudi/Germany **might** beat US on short payback even if nominal RPI is lower. **The 75% share and CPI values are scenarios, not observed app economics.**

### Prior benchmark notes (re-verify before investor use)

- **RevenueCat 2026:** reported ~115K+ subscription apps and ~$16B+ revenue analyzed; ~4.6% of new subscription apps reach $10K monthly revenue within two years. Exact benchmark population and date matter. [State of Subscription Apps](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps).
- Prior cited **North America D14 median RPI ~$0.38, D60 ~$0.55**; hard-paywall D14 median ~$2.32 and D60 ~$3.09; top-quartile hard-paywall outcomes were substantially higher. These are **cross-category distribution statistics**, not Cleaner/TV Remote achieved cohorts.
- Prior cited global app-store D60 RPI **iOS ~$0.42 versus Google Play ~$0.16**; payer LTVs closer (**iOS ~$23.38 vs Android ~$21.62** at the referenced horizon). The conversion gap can be larger than the value gap among payers.
- **Adapty Apple Ads** past benchmarks: US overall cost/download ~$2.51 in one dataset; another country-median report from **AppTweak** puts US Search Results CPI at ~$4.06. **These are different samples/definitions**, not direct contradictions; use the chosen source consistently.
- Global Apple Ads niche examples previously reported: Remote Control ~$0.56; VPN ~$0.60; Document Scanner ~$3.74; PDF Reader ~$4.31; study/exam-prep terms can be much higher. **Do not label these US-specific.** [Adapty report](https://adapty.io/blog/apple-ads-benchmarks-2026/).
- Early planning US iOS Meta CPI for broad utilities was sometimes modeled ~$4–7, and a later financial workbook used **$5–9 for Cleaner**. Neither is an authoritative category benchmark. Run ads.
- An older Sept workbook modeled TV Remote gross D30 RPI ~$2.50, Storage Cleaner ~$3.50, **not public observed cohorts**. The workbook also has modeled US channel CPIs. Use it as a scenario tool, not empirical evidence.
- Weekly subscription products can front-load D30 revenue but increase refund/retention/trust risk. Test transparent weekly, monthly and annual prices; annual cash collection can speed payback while changing longer-term renewal patterns.

### Qualification ladder / spending rules

| CRR30 after fees/refunds/variable cost | Prior recommended action |
|---|---|
| <0.60 | Stop scaling; diagnose/rebuild or kill |
| 0.60–0.80 | Product, funnel and paywall work only |
| 0.80–0.95 | Limited additional controlled experiments |
| 0.95–1.10 | Begin careful marginal scaling |
| 1.10–1.30 | Strong winner candidate |
| >1.30 | Scale progressively until incremental economics deteriorate |

**Important qualification:** thresholds above are working policy, not guarantees. They assume the cohort matures, revenue is appropriately netted, refund lag is accounted for, retention is credible, and cash settlements support the spending cadence. Also check marginal ROAS, payback by country/channel, churn and cohort mix—not just one 30-day number.

### Early Cleaner launch metrics proposed (targets, not industry facts)

- Photo permission consent >70–75% where full-library access is actually needed.
- Successful scan >90% of users granting usable permission.
- Meaningful first-session cleanup >25–30% of activated users, with segment control for available clutter.
- Install→paid ~6% as initial strong target, ~8%+ excellent **if economically compatible**.
- D7 gross RPI >$1.5–2 and D30 gross RPI >$3–3.5 depending CPI and market.
- Refunds ideally under ~5–7%, rating ≥4.5, first renewal and Month-2 retention sufficiently healthy.
- Workbook alternative higher target set: install→trial ≥18%, trial→paid ≥40%, D7 net RPI ≥$2.00, D30 ≥$2.60. **These are aggressive winner-case gates from a separate modeling snapshot and should not be presented as validated market medians.**

## 7. Country strategy: iOS versus Android

1. One or two winners frequently dominate portfolio revenue.

5. Some companies evolved from build to acquire or into adjacent media businesses; no single sequence is mandatory.
6. Revenue multiples alone are dangerous if subscriber churn, paid-UA reinvestment or gross margin make cash flows fragile.

### 13.2 SAFE: what it is

A **Simple Agreement for Future Equity** is an instrument where an investor supplies money now and receives rights to equity in connection with a later trigger, typically a priced financing. It is **generally not ordinary debt**: usually no contractual maturity or interest, but terms differ. It is also not the same as immediately purchasing a priced share class.

- **Post-money valuation cap:** a $1M investment at a **$10M post-money SAFE cap** corresponds approximately to 10% *prior to additional dilutive issuances* under standard simplifying assumptions; model actual terms and financing conversion.
- **Pre-money vs post-money cap** changes dilution math. Always ask which.
- Discount, MFN, pro-rata rights and liquidity/dissolution terms matter.
- A SAFE in a US-style structure is not automatically suitable for an Indian entity; obtain India/US counsel on incorporation, FEMA/FDI, shareholder instruments, ESOP grants, cross-border payments and securities/tax compliance.
- Legal costs, formation, fundraise closing, and potential IP-holding-company choices are not fully modeled in historic budgets.

## 16. Cross-sell, alternative monetization and optional models

### 16.1 Cross-promotion

- Cross-promote logically related families after enough users exist: Cleaner → Video Compression/Photo Organization/Screenshot Organizer/Private Vault; Docs → Scan/OCR/PDF/Sign/Fax; Communication → Second Number/Spam Detection/Translation.
- Earlier research cited gaming cross-promo and non-gaming cross-sell case studies with materially cheaper effective CPI and/or retention uplift, but **do not assume gaming results transfer** to utilities without tests.
- Opportunity cost: promoting another app consumes attention that may otherwise improve core revenue/retention.
- Bundle only when adjacent apps provide legitimate value; an oversized bundle should not be a justification for aggressive pricing.

### 16.2 Beyond subscriptions

Previously explored: one-time/lifetime IAP, usage-based pricing (per fax, per AI generation), ads where compatible with UX and buyer value, referral/affiliate revenue when transparent, bundles/cross-sell, and B2B/white-label/partnership extensions. **Model each on contribution margin, platform compliance, retention and customer value**, not only headline gross sales.

### 16.3 Alternative distribution / app-store fees

A prior line of inquiry considered PWAs/web-to-app and Android APK sideloading to avoid mobile platform commissions after acquiring highly retained users. This should **not** become the assumed launch model without legal, platform-policy and friction verification: App Store steering/payment rules differ by country and change, sideloading outside iOS is platform-specific, converting retained users to a different install is high-friction, and migrating subscription entitlements/auto-renewals can create support and compliance problems. Test legal web checkout where permitted; never treat “avoid 30%” as a universal right.

## 20. Distilled operating principles

1. **Distribution and conversion are the business:** AI makes software cheaper to build, not cheap to acquire users for.
2. **The category is proven; our company isn't** until cohorts show attractive net contribution.
3. **Build launch-grade trust, not thin feature parity**; on device/photo/telecom/document claims must be genuine.
4. **Month-2 retention matters** even when D30 payback is the capital-allocation gate.
5. **Gross revenue is not net CRR.** Platform take, refunds, cost and settlement must be included.
6. **Country choice is a unit-economics question**; never simply copy US budgets across 12 storefronts.
7. **Android is a separate business model**, not a low-ARPU afterthought or automatic port.
8. **Multiple apps are a search process; the winner deserves concentration.**
9. **A true cofounder and a senior employee are different deals.** Zero salary materially shifts equity, but commitments/vesting/governance still matter.
10. **A $1M seed check buys time to learn, not a guarantee.** Preserve capital when no app clears the gate; scale only demonstrated marginal economics.
11. **Maintain a dated evidence ledger.** Every provisional benchmark, deck market number and valuation proposal can expire.
