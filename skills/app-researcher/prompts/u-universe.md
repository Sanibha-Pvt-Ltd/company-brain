# Stage U — Universe discovery (before stage 0)

Run once, early, to decide **which categories deserve a lens**. Output: `members/<member>/drafts/universe.md` (admin promotes to `research/universe.md`, then fills `company/categories`). Web data only; no paid tools yet. Follow the evidence discipline in SKILL.md: tag every claim, source + date on every number, `[UNKNOWN]` over invention, ranges over false precision, and when two sources disagree report the spread.

**Frame.** The question is not "what is big?" but "what should Sanibha build?" — a ~4-person iOS studio, US App Store, ~6-week MVP, strong on execution/ASO/paid growth, no deep tech. Work backwards from **$1M/month net of Apple's fee**. Large market ≠ attractive startup market; proven demand from incumbents ≠ opportunity; subscription revenue ≠ good economics (count churn, refunds); LTV alone is not enough — D7/D30 cash recovery matters; novelty is not a plus. Exclude any business whose economics only work through deceptive billing. iOS only.

## 1. Taxonomy
List 30–50 subcategories of consumer utility/productivity apps, grouped: productivity (todo, calendar, notes, PKM, habits, focus, time/goals, journaling, email, meetings, scheduling, docs/PDF, scanning, files, AI assistants, writing, transcription, keyboards); device utilities (storage/duplicate cleaners, battery, VPN, security/privacy, passwords, caller ID/spam, second number, compression, transfer, QR, ad blockers, network, widgets/customization); personal utilities (budgeting, expenses, subscription/bill tracking, price tracking, shopping, travel, mileage, receipts, family/shared lists, home inventory, medication, meal/grocery); AI-native (categories newly possible via generative/multimodal AI, vision, speech, on-device models). Add categories missed. Mark each: mature-large / small-fast-growing / emerging (no reliable data).

## 2. Size and winners (per attractive subcategory)
US and global revenue and downloads (annual), growth, number of meaningful competitors, top-3/top-10 concentration, avg price, free→paid conversion, primary monetization (subscription / one-time / ads / affiliate / transaction / lead-gen / hybrid). Then a dataset of ≥50 major or fast-growing apps: company, app, category, launch year, downloads, revenue, growth, price, trial structure, rating and count, acquisition channels (organic vs paid), per-channel viability (Meta, Google, TikTok, influencer, SEO, ASO), referral loops, usage frequency. Say which fields are `[ESTIMATE:<source>]` and show the math for modeled values.

Also find: (a) "boring" quiet cash-generators — simple, unglamorous, highly searched, subscription-driven, small teams (scanners, cleaners, PDF tools, fax, caller ID, second-number, converters, widgets, keyboard tools, niche calculators, trackers); (b) app factories — owners of many utility apps (apps, est. revenue, acquisition and dev strategy, team size, shared tech, cross-promo) — and say whether a portfolio beats one big app; (c) breakout apps from the last 3–5 years that grew to $10M+/$30M+/$50M+/$100M+ and what wedge they used that incumbents missed.

## 3. Economics (top 20–30 apps)
Acquisition: CPI, CAC (trial / subscriber), organic share, channels, creative formats and ad angles (Meta Ad Library, TikTok Creative Center, YouTube, Google, influencer, affiliate, SEO, ASO). Monetization: trial start and trial→paid, install→paid, monthly/annual price and plan mix, ARPU/ARPPU, refund rate, net after Apple fees. Retention: D1/D7/D30/M3/M6/M12 and subscriber retention; separate naturally high retention from retention produced by dark patterns.

## 4. $1M/month model (per promising category)
For $5, $10, $20, $30, $50/month and $60, $100, $200/year, compute gross and net-of-fees: subscribers needed, installs, trials, conversion, churn, monthly replacement users, CAC, acquisition spend, contribution margin. Model ad-led apps where relevant. Show which categories make $1M/month structurally easiest.

**D7 economics:** revenue per new user at D0/D1/D3/D7/D30/D90/D365; D7 revenue ÷ CAC and D7 contribution ÷ CAC. Bucket: A ≥100% recovered by D7 · B 50–100% · C <50% but retention/LTV compensates. Explain why (annual upfront, trials, weekly plans, upsells, paywalls, high-intent search, affiliate, ads, bundles).

## 5. Scoring dimensions (1–10, each with a one-line evidence cite)
Intrinsic retention · usage frequency · breadth of demand (can one person use several such apps?) · paid-acquisition scalability (Meta, TikTok, Google Search/App Campaigns [iOS = Apple Ads], YouTube, influencer, affiliate; 3-second ad clarity, urgency, visual demo, before/after, emotion, fear/loss, measurable outcome) · Creative Velocity (how many materially different ad concepts) · search intent (Google/App Store volume, CPC, SEO/ASO difficulty, user already knows the job) · time to value (install → core benefit; <2 min is best) · data generation (frequency, usefulness, defensibility, cross-sell) · App Store/regulatory risk (rejection, refunds, subscription scrutiny, privacy, ATT, VPN rules, photo/contact/health/financial permissions) · build complexity (team, difficulty, time, backend, AI cost, third-party APIs, licensing; where AI coding tools cut cost) · defensibility type (tech / data / brand / distribution / paid skill / SEO / ASO / creative / bundling / pricing / velocity — favour markets where distribution and performance marketing beat deep IP).

Also mine complaints on leaders (App Store reviews, Reddit, YouTube comments, forums), clustered into pricing, subscription, UX, reliability, missing features, privacy, ads, support, performance, accuracy, interoperability — prefer categories where users want the product but dislike the options. Check non-subscription monetization (ads, affiliate, lead-gen, commerce, referrals) and bundle vs single-utility vs portfolio-of-small-apps.

## 6. Opportunity score /100 (default weights; if `research/scoring.md` exists, use it and say so)
Market size 8 · growth 5 · reach $1M/month 10 · paid scalability 12 · CAC attractiveness 8 · D7 recovery 12 · LTV/CAC 8 · intrinsic retention 7 · frequency 5 · time to value 5 · creative potential 5 · search intent 4 · build simplicity 4 · competitive intensity 3 · platform risk 2 · data generation 2. Commentary may not override the score without an explicit reason.

## 7. Specific products, not categories
For the top 10: customer, trigger, problem, exact product, 60-second first experience, ad hook, pricing, channels, economics (CPI, install→trial, trial→paid, CAC, D7/D30 revenue, 12-month LTV, gross margin, LTV/CAC), scale (subscribers, installs/month and spend for $1M/month under conservative/base/aggressive). For each: 3–5 closest competitors (revenue, downloads, price, rating, strength, weakness, acquisition, our wedge) and **why the incumbent hasn't captured it** — no convincing answer = penalize. For the final 5 build a full sensitivity model (installs, subscribers, revenue, Apple + payment fees, AI/API costs, marketing, support, contribution profit, EBITDA before overhead): would $1M/month actually be a good business?
